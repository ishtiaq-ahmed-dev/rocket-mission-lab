MODEL NOTES · PUBLIC-DATA, NOT CERTIFIED

Independent educational model, not affiliated with, endorsed by, or derived from SpaceX flight data. Every figure here is simulated, not a vehicle specification or a flight-certification claim.

REFERENCE ANCHORS

  • Public Falcon 9 Block 5 figures: 549,054 kg published liftoff mass, nine Merlin 1D engines at 845 kN sea level / 914 kN vacuum, 282/311 s, and one MVac at 981 kN / 348 s. The model's no-payload mass closes to 546.6 t, 0.45% below the published figure; dry mass and drag coefficients are modeled.
  • WGS-84 GM and rotation, a spherical mean Earth radius, ISO 2533:1975 / U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976 layers, and Mach-dependent drag. The reference profile is data/reference/international-standard.json.

FLIGHT CONTROL · NEURAL POLICY

  • Every command in this flight comes from a trained neural network evaluated at 10 Hz on the vehicle's own state: engine ignition, cutoff and count, throttle, attitude, stage separation, second-stage burn, fairing jettison and payload deploy. There is no scripted guidance and no fallback controller in this page.
  • The network was trained by imitation of a classical teacher with DAgger (scripts/flight_policy/). On 300 unseen dispersed flights (wind, thrust, drag and mass) it completed 299 missions; the teacher also completed 299 of the same flights.
  • Return profile after Falcon 9 RTLS practice: cold-gas flip, three-engine boostback with horizontal thrust (never toward the ground), unpowered ballistic coast with engines leading, three-engine entry burn of about ten seconds near 66 km, unpowered grid-fin glide, single-engine landing burn lit at the last feasible moment.
  • After separation the booster still climbs on its own momentum while gravity slows it, and falls after apogee. Stage separation conserves momentum; an engine finishes its shutdown before it can relight.

NOT MODELLED / NOT CERTIFIED

  • 6-DOF rigid-body dynamics (attitude is a rate-limited first-order response), propellant slosh, aeroelasticity, sensor noise, abort modes, weather, FAA/range safety and structural or thermal limits.
  • Plume length and width follow the ambient pressure ratio; soot, grid-fin and leg motion are illustrative. This is not a validated real-world flight model.
